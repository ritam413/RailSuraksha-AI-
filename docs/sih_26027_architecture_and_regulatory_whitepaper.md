## Executive Summary

Indian Railways (IR) operates over 13,000 passenger trains and 8,000 freight trains daily across a 68,000 km route network. Maintaining track geometry, overhead equipment (OHE), and signaling infrastructure requires scheduled track possessions known as **maintenance blocks**. Currently, block planning across Indian Railways is largely decentralized, manual, and siloed within individual railway divisions.

Each engineering department—Civil (TMS), Electrical TRD (TDMS), and Signaling & Telecom (SMMS)—requests separate blocks through legacy portals or ad-hoc paperwork. Section Controllers manually reconcile these requests against live train paths using the Control Office Application (COA). This manual workflow creates three critical systemic failure modes:

1. **Corridor Fragmentation:** Independent blocks on adjacent sections compound train delays rather than aligning downtime.
2. **Sub-Optimal Asset Utilization:** Track maintenance machines (e.g., Duomatic tampers, ballast cleaners) idle between isolated possessions.
3. **Safety Disconnect:** Temporary Speed Restrictions (TSRs) generated by maintenance activities require manual caution order issuance, creating potential failure points in driver compliance.

**IRIS AI (Intelligent Railway Inspection and Restoration AI)** solves these structural bottlenecks by introducing an automated, multi-department optimization engine. Auto-BDMS pools maintenance defects from TMS, TDMS, and SMMS, evaluates corridor availability in COA, and executes a **Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP)** model to generate coordinated **Joint Shadow Blocks**. Furthermore, it integrates with **RDSO Kavach TCAS** (`RDSO/SPN/196/2020`) to electronically transmit Temporary Speed Restrictions directly to locomotive cabs.

## 2. Multi-Department Joint Shadow Blocking

### 2.1 The Shadow Block Concept
In traditional operations, if the Civil Department secures a 3-hour block on Track Section A, the Electrical and Signaling departments may not be notified in time to mobilize. Days later, Electrical takes another 2.5-hour block on the exact same line.

**Shadow Blocking** identifies the primary constraint (e.g., a major 4-hour track relaying possession) and dynamically schedules secondary and tertiary maintenance tasks from other directorates within the spatial and temporal shadow of the primary block.

```
TRADITIONAL SILOED MODEL:
Day 1: [--- Civil Tamping: 3.5 hrs ---] (Electrical & Signal idle)
Day 3:                                    [--- OHE Inspection: 2.5 hrs ---] (Civil idle)
Day 5:                                                                       [--- Signal Overhaul: 2.0 hrs ---]
Total Corridor Disruption: 8.0 Hours

IRIS AI JOINT SHADOW-BLOCK MODEL:
Day 1: ┌─── PRIMARY: Civil Track Relay (4.0 hrs) ──────────────────────────┐
       ├─── SHADOW 1: Electrical OHE Wire Adjustment (3.0 hrs concurrent) ──┤
       └─── SHADOW 2: S&T Point Machine Lubrication (1.5 hrs concurrent) ───┘
Total Corridor Disruption: 4.0 Hours (50% Reduction in Lost Line Capacity)
```

## 3. Mathematical Optimization Formulation (MILP)

Auto-BDMS solves block scheduling as a Mixed-Integer Linear Program (MILP) using Google OR-Tools:

### 3.1 Objective Function
$$\min Z = \alpha \sum_{i \in B} \text{TrainDelayCost}(i) + \beta \sum_{j \in D} \text{PendingRiskPenalty}(j) - \gamma \sum_{k \in S} \text{ShadowBlockSynergy}(k)$$

Where:
* $B$ is the set of scheduled maintenance blocks.
* $D$ is the set of unserved infrastructure defects across TMS, TDMS, and SMMS.
* $S$ is the set of co-located joint blocks bundled together.
* $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ are operational weights tuned for network congestion:
  * $\alpha = 0.45$: Cost of passenger and freight delay minutes.
  * $\beta = 0.40$: Safety risk factor for deferred P1/P2 maintenance.
  * $\gamma = 0.15$: Multi-department coordination reward.

### 3.2 Hard Operational Constraints

1. **Headway & Corridor Window:**
   $$\text{BlockStartTime}(b) \ge \text{PassageTime}(\text{PrecedingTrain}) + T_{\text{safety\_headway}}$$
2. **Machine & Crew Contiguity:**
   A track tamper or OHE tower wagon cannot be assigned to two distant stations without transit time:
   $$\text{StartTime}(b_{m, 2}) \ge \text{EndTime}(b_{m, 1}) + \text{TransitTime}(\text{Loc}_1, \text{Loc}_2)$$
3. **Safety Interlocking Lockout:**
   $$\text{TrackCircuitStatus}(\text{Section}_k) = \text{OCCUPIED} \implies \text{SignalAspect}(\text{EntrySignal}) = \text{RED}$$

## 5. RDSO Regulatory & Kavach TCAS Compliance

### 5.1 Regulatory Framework
Auto-BDMS strictly complies with Indian Railways statutory manuals and specifications:

1. **Indian Railways Permanent Way Manual (IRPWM - 2020 Edition):**
   * Regulates permissible track geometry tolerances, USFD flaw classification, and mandatory speed reductions post-maintenance.
2. **AC Traction Manual (ACTM - Volume II):**
   * Prescribes OHE power block protocols and electrical earthing clearances.
3. **Indian Railways Signal Engineering Manual (IRSEM - 2021 Edition):**
   * Dictates interlock testing requirements and axle counter isolation procedures.
4. **RDSO Kavach TCAS Specification (`RDSO/SPN/196/2020`):**
   * Defines direct wireless communication between trackside Radio Block Centers (RBC), Stationary Kavach, and Onboard Locomotive Kavach units.

### 5.2 Emergency Braking Distance (EBD) Physics
When maintenance requires a speed restriction (TSR), Auto-BDMS computes the exact deceleration envelope using the RDSO Kavach physical braking model:

$$D_{\text{stop}} = \frac{V^2}{2g(\mu + G)} + V \cdot t_{\text{reaction}}$$

Where:
* $V$ = Locomotive velocity ($\text{m/s}$)
* $g$ = Gravitational acceleration ($9.81\text{ m/s}^2$)
* $\mu$ = Dynamic track friction coefficient (Monsoon Wet: $0.095$, Fog: $0.115$, Dry: $0.134$)
* $G$ = Track gradient (e.g., $+0.002$ for rising gradient, $-0.003$ for falling)
* $t_{\text{reaction}}$ = Kavach brake system latency ($1.2\text{ s}$ autonomous, $2.5\text{ s}$ advisory)

### 5.3 Explainable AI Audit Dossier (RDSO Form 14B)
To ensure compliance with Commissioner of Railway Safety (CRS) guidelines, every automated intervention is immutably logged across 4 chronological stages with an SHA-256 digital verification seal:

1. **Stage 1 (Vision / Ingestion):** Hazard / Defect detection timestamp and raw telemetry.
2. **Stage 2 (Kinematics / Headway):** Speed, distance to asset, and line availability.
3. **Stage 3 (Physics / MILP Evaluation):** Stopping distance envelope and corridor cost balance.
4. **Stage 4 (Actuation / Sanction):** Kavach solenoid trigger or Section Controller sanction.
